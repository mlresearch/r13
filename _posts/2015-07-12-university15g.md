abstract: A variety of advanced statistics are used to evaluate player actions in
  the National Hockey League, but they fail to account for the context in which an
  action occurs or to look ahead to the long-term effects of an action. We apply the
  Markov Game formalism to develop a novel approach to valuing player actions that
  incorporates context and lookahead. Dynamic programming is used to learn Q-functions
  that quantify the impact of actions on goal scoring resp. penalties. Learning is
  based on a massive dataset that contains over 2.8M events in the National Hockey
  League. The impact of player actions is found to vary widely depending on the context,
  with possible positive and negative effects for the same action. We show that lookahead
  makes a substantial difference to the action impact scores. Players are ranked according
  to the aggregate impact of their actions. We compare this impact ranking with previous
  player metrics, such as plus-minus, total points, and salary.

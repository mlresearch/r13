abstract: Many modern information access problems involve highly complex patterns
  that cannot be handled by traditional keyword based search. Active Search is an
  emerging paradigm that helps users quickly find relevant information by efficiently
  collecting and learning from user feedback. We consider active search on graphs,
  where the nodes represent the set of instances users want to search over and the
  edges encode pairwise similarity among the instances. Existing active search algorithms
  are either short of theoretical guarantees or inadequate for graph data. Motivated
  by recent advances in active learning on graphs, namely the Sigma-optimality selection
  criterion, we propose new active search algorithms suitable for graphs with theoretical
  guarantees and demonstrate their effectiveness on several real-world datasets.

abstract: The key for effective interaction in many multiagent applications is to
  reason explicitly about the behaviour of other agents, in the form of a hypothesised
  behaviour. While there exist several methods for the construction of a behavioural
  hypothesis, there is currently no universal theory which would allow an agent to
  contemplate the correctness of a hypothesis. In this work, we present an novel algorithm
  which decides this question in the form of a frequentist hypothesis test. The algorithm
  allows for multiple metrics in the construction of the test statistic and learns
  its distribution during the interaction process, with asymptotic correctness guarantees.
  We present results from a comprehensive set of experiments, demonstrating that the
  algorithm achieves high accuracy and scalability at low computational costs.
